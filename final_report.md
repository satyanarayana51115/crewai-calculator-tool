# Breakthrough in Green Chemistry: New Catalyst Achieves Landmark 96% Plastic Degradation Rate

In a world grappling with the environmental toll of synthetic waste, researchers have long been searching for an efficient way to accelerate the breakdown of complex polymers. This week, a team of chemical engineers published a groundbreaking study that marks a massive leap forward in green chemistry.

At the heart of their publication is a singular, defining calculation result that has the scientific community buzzing: **96**.

According to the newly released data, the team’s newly synthesized bio-catalyst successfully degraded polyethylene terephthalate (PET) plastics by an astonishing **96%** within a mere 48-hour cycle. Previous methods struggled to cross the 50% threshold under similar mild, eco-friendly conditions, often requiring extreme heat or toxic co-solvents.

The calculation was reached after rigorous multi-variable testing, measuring the ratio of decomposed polymer mass against the initial structural integrity. The resulting figure of 96% represents not just a statistical triumph, but a viable pathway toward industrial-scale recycling. 

"We knew the catalyst was efficient, but hitting the 96% mark under standard room temperature conditions exceeded our most optimistic models," said Dr. Elena Rostova, lead author of the study. 

With this computational and experimental proof in hand, the researchers are now looking to scale the technology for municipal waste management facilities. If these numbers hold true on a larger scale, the global approach to recycling could be transformed forever.
